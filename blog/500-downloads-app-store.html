500 Downloads on the App Store

We just crossed ~500 downloads on the App Store. No ads, no viral moment, no influencer push — just word of mouth and a belief that private communication and crypto identity should be out of the box.

When I first submitted Viper to the App Store, I wasn't sure it would get approved. A messenger that doesn't ask for your phone number, uses encrypted messages out of the box (server stores only encrypted content), and uses TON's ADNL protocol under the hood — it's not exactly the typical consumer app.

But it got approved. And people started downloading it.

What 500 means

500 isn't a big number by any metric that matters to growth hackers or investors. But it means 500 people actively chose a messenger that puts cryptographic identity ahead of convenience. That's not nothing.

These are early adopters in the truest sense — people who are willing to share a public key instead of a phone number, who understand why end-to-end encryption at the protocol level matters, and who want something different.

Where we go from here

We're not chasing downloads. We're building the right thing. Each update to Viper makes it faster, more reliable, and more private. The recent 1.2 release brought end-to-end encrypted reactions — even emoji responses stay between you and the person you're talking to.

If you haven't tried it yet, download Viper on the App Store.

And if you're already using it — thank you. You're part of something that matters.

A note on TON Wallet

Viper was built with TON Wallet integration in mind — encrypted messages tied to your on-chain identity felt like the natural next step. We implemented it. Then Apple rejected it.

According to App Store rules, only registered organisations can integrate crypto wallets — not indie developers. So for now, that feature stays on the shelf. It's a frustrating reminder that even privacy-first software has to play by platform rules.

We're not giving up on it. But we're being honest about where things stand.
