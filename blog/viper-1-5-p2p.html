Viper 1.5 — Device-to-Device Communication Is Coming

Updated for Viper 1.5.34 (November 19, 2026)

What changed since this post. Where the post and the app differ, the app is right.

  • P2P shipped in 1.5 and is described in How your messages travel. Direct links need both of you in each other's contacts; by default the relay goes first.
  • The Enterprise part of this post was not re-checked against the App Store app.

Since Viper's first release, messages have been routed through relays — a deliberate architectural choice that keeps infrastructure costs low and simplifies connectivity across networks and firewalls. Relays never see message content, but they do sit in the path.

With Viper 1.5, that changes.

Real Device-to-Device Communication

Viper 1.5 introduces true peer-to-peer (P2P) messaging. When two devices can establish a direct connection, messages travel between them without touching any relay at all. The relay network remains available as a fallback for difficult network conditions — but when P2P is possible, it's used.

This means:

  • Lower latency. No relay hop means faster message delivery on local networks, same Wi-Fi, or direct internet connections.
  • Zero relay involvement. When P2P is active, no infrastructure sits between sender and recipient — not ours, not yours.
  • Same encryption. The E2E encryption model is unchanged. P2P doesn't weaken the cryptographic guarantees — if anything, removing the relay from the path reduces the attack surface.

P2P in Viper Enterprise

For enterprise deployments, P2P is a significant addition. Teams operating on the same internal network — an office, a data center, a corporate LAN — can communicate directly, with no traffic leaving the local environment at all.

This enables:

  • Fully offline internal messaging. A local deployment with no internet dependency for intra-office communication.
  • Reduced relay load. Internal traffic no longer routes through the relay, freeing relay capacity for external connections.
  • Air-gapped segments. Departments or devices on isolated network segments can communicate directly over P2P without relay involvement, strengthening network isolation.

P2P becomes another layer in the enterprise network topology model — alongside relay-based routing, it gives infrastructure teams more granular control over how messages move across their environment.

What Stays the Same

P2P is additive. The relay fallback remains. Encryption remains. The identity model remains. If P2P cannot be established, Viper falls back to relay routing transparently — the user experience is unchanged.
