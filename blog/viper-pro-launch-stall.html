Viper Pro: A Launch, a Stall, and a Week Lost

Updated for Viper 1.5.34 (November 19, 2026)

What changed since this post. Where the post and the app differ, the app is right.

  • Direct P2P links are still contacts-only: a link opens only when each of you has the other in contacts.
  • Anon Mode today: you are out of Search and your chats keep working (the 1.5.16 text said unreachable to anyone you have not chatted with).
  • “Messages from Non-Contacts” is a free switch (on by default); with it off, messages from everyone else are dropped quietly.
  • Viper Pro is a monthly App Store subscription; one renewing subscription per Apple ID works on one account. See Viper Pro.

Viper Pro was supposed to be the moment the project became sustainable. Instead, 1.5.16 shipped, sat in review for a week, and by the time 1.5.17 made payments actually work, the launch window was already gone.

What 1.5.16 Was Supposed to Be

The release had two halves.

The product half: Viper Pro — a monthly subscription.

The privacy half: direct P2P connections are now contacts-only. People you haven't added can't connect to your device directly. Files from non-contacts are always blocked. And for Pro users, Anonymous Mode — you become unreachable to anyone you haven't chatted with before.

In dev, all of it worked. Subscriptions resolved, the paywall rendered, restore worked, the privacy toggles enforced. I shipped it.

What Actually Happened

Apple didn't reject the subscriptions. They just didn't approve them. The build went live, the privacy changes landed, but the in-app purchases sat in review — for a week.

Users opened Viper Pro and saw nothing to buy. A subscription launch with no way to subscribe.

The Hot Version

There's no way to nudge a stuck review from the app side. The only lever is another binary. So I shipped a hot build — not because anything in the app was broken, but because a new submission was the fastest path to getting the purchases re-examined.

It's an expensive way to get attention: a review cycle, a version bump, a release note, all to re-trigger a queue that should have moved on its own.

1.5.17: Payments, Finally

1.5.17 is smart restore purchases. The purchases got approved. Payments became available. The paywall works end to end now — subscribe, restore, the subscription state is correct.

The Cost

The feature works. The lesson is that works in dev and works in prod are different sentences when a third party controls the checkout.

A week of dead air at the exact moment you're trying to convert interest into revenue is a momentum loss you don't get back. People who opened the app to subscribe found nothing, and most of them didn't return a week later to try again.

Viper Pro is live. It just arrived a week late.
