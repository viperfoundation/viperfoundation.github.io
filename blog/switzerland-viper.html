Viper discovered Switzerland

A week ago I was sitting at a bar in Zurich. Viper open on my phone. Feldschlösschen glasses around. I didn't expect to be there.

That trip made me think seriously about Switzerland as a target market.

Switzerland is not the EU

This matters more than it sounds.

Viper is currently available on the App Store — but not in Europe. The EU's Digital Markets Act and App Store regulations have created a complicated environment for small apps. Compliance overhead is real.

Switzerland is not in the EU. It operates under its own regulatory framework. Which means Viper is accessible there today, without the legal friction that blocks European distribution.

That's not a workaround — that's a market signal.

Switzerland and privacy: not just a stereotype

Everyone knows the clichés: Swiss banks, banking secrecy, discretion. But underneath those clichés is something real.

Privacy is a cultural value there, not just a legal checkbox.

People in Switzerland are genuinely skeptical of surveillance, data collection, and centralized platforms. ProtonMail is Swiss. Threema — the most privacy-focused mainstream messenger in Europe — is Swiss. That's not a coincidence.

Threema as a reference point

Threema has ~12 million users. It costs money (one-time purchase). It's not flashy. It works.

It succeeded in Switzerland and DACH region precisely because people there will pay for privacy. They understand the value proposition.

Viper's architecture is different from Threema's: cryptographic identity lives with the user, and the underlying protocol is built on TON's ADNL. But to be honest — messaging still flows through relay servers. Without them, you can't reach the network.

The real potential is federation: anyone who understands the protocol can run their own relay. That's the direction — independent relayers, not a single point of trust. We're not fully there yet, but the foundation exists.

What that evening actually means

I was sitting in Zurich with Viper open. No grand plan. Just testing the app in the wild.

Organic discovery. Word of mouth. No paid acquisition.

That's how Telegram spread in Eastern Europe. That's how Signal spread after WhatsApp's privacy policy change. Small pockets of early adopters who actually care about the thing.

Switzerland might be one of those pockets.

The thesis

Switzerland checks every box for Viper's early market:

— Available on App Store (not EU, no DMA friction) — Strong privacy culture, not just regulation — High purchasing power, willing to pay for quality — Existing precedent: Threema, ProtonMail — Small enough to focus on, large enough to matter — German, French, Italian — multilingual, internationally connected

We didn't plan Switzerland. But maybe that's exactly why it's interesting.

The best markets find you before you find them.
