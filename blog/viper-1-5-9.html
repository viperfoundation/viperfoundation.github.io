Viper 1.5.9 — Ephemeral Relay, Local Contacts & Persistent P2P History

Updated for Viper 1.5.34 (November 19, 2026)

What changed since this post. Where the post and the app differ, the app is right.

  • Still true: the relay keeps a message only until it is delivered, then deletes it, and keeps no history. Chats are stored on your device, encrypted.
  • Correction: the contact list is kept on your device, encrypted, but the app still sends each contact you add to the relay (your public key → their public key, with the name encrypted to you). So the relay does see who your contacts are.
  • New since: Encrypted Chat Backup (Settings → Security and Data) to take chats to a new phone.

Viper 1.5.9 is a privacy-focused release: messages leave the server as soon as they reach you, contacts never leave your device, and P2P history now survives app restarts.

Ephemeral Relay Storage

Relay messages are no longer stored permanently on the server.

When you're online, messages are routed straight to your device — they never touch persistent storage at all. When you're offline, the relay holds incoming messages temporarily, just long enough to deliver them. The moment you come back online, messages are forwarded to you and immediately removed from the server.

The relay never accumulates your message history. There's nothing to subpoena, nothing to breach.

Contacts Stored on Device

Your contact list is now stored entirely on your device. It no longer syncs through or lives on any server. Your social graph is yours — private by default, inaccessible to anyone else.

Persistent P2P Message History

P2P messages are now saved locally and survive app restarts. Previously, closing the app meant losing your direct P2P conversation history. Now it's stored on-device and available the next time you open Viper — just like relay-delivered messages.
